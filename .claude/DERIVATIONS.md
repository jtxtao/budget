# DERIVATIONS.md

The eleven cross-store hooks and the pure modules behind them. `CLAUDE.md` carries the envelope identity itself; the five conditions that keep it true are here, with everything else each derivation owns. **Read the section for an area before changing its maths.**

The eleven, so the count is checkable rather than folklore: `useEnvelopes`, `useAccountBalances`, `useNetWorth`, `useRetirementProjection`, `useSpendingReport`, `useGiving`, `useSavingsGoalEnvelopes`, `useEmergencyFund`, `useCardOffers`, `useCardValue`, `useLoans`. Each owns a definition no single store can own.

Not in the eleven: `useDashboard` and `useStartingBalances` (they arrange or re-window what `useEnvelopes` already derived), `usePlanHealth` (it crosses three stores but never reads the books at all), `useNextPaycheck` (one store), `useRecordCashback` (a writer, not a derivation), and `useUpcoming` — also one store, and it **must not become** a cross-store hook: a schedule is a prediction, and a hook that read the ledger would invite matching an occurrence against a transaction that looks like it, which is guesswork about intent and wrong the first time a bill is paid twice on purpose.

## Envelopes (`src/hooks/useEnvelopes.js`)

`CLAUDE.md` carries the formulas and the invariant. This is what keeps them true.

### The two terms that are money still there and no longer free

Each is subtracted from the pool and reported back, so the identity closes over it:

- **`reserved` is the emergency fund held back** (`emergencyReservedCents`, from `useEmergencyFund`'s `reservedCents`, capped at the target where there is one, never below zero). Without it that money read as "to be assigned" and could be given to groceries. Only from the current month on, and off when `emergencyFund.holdBack` is false.
- **`assignedAhead` is a month funded before it arrived** (`assignedAheadCents`) — budgeting ahead, YNAB's and Actual's. **An assignment, unlike a transaction, may be dated ahead on purpose**: it stays out of every figure belonging to the month on screen (it is not this month's `assignedCents`, it seeds no row, it is not in `carriedIn` until its month is behind), but it cannot stay out of the pool — the money it was given is today's money, and a pool that offered it again would let one dollar fund two months. Floored at zero, `reserved`'s rule, because a negative total ahead means a later month will *release* money that is sitting in its envelope today, where `available` already counts it.
- **`forgivenOverspend` is what a no-rollover category's overspending cost the pool instead of the category** (`forgivenOverspendCents`). `budgets[].rolloverEnabled === false` switches that one category's `carriedInCents` from the flat closed-form sum to a chronological walk over its own dated history (folding in whatever it did undated as the starting point, since undated movement has no boundary of its own): the running total is floored at zero at every period boundary it crosses *strictly before* `P`, never at `P`'s own — a month it is negative within still shows that, the app's one definition of trouble, and only the *next* month starts clean. Every cent a floor discards is real spending that really happened and cannot just vanish from the identity, so it is summed, across every opted-out category, into this term and subtracted from the pool exactly as `assignedAhead` is: the household is choosing to have the deficit covered automatically rather than carried as a debt against the category. Already non-negative by construction (each correction is `-running` at the moment `running` is negative), so there is no floor to state separately. Zero whenever no category has opted out, which is what keeps every existing figure — and the tripwire — exactly as they were.

`ToBeAssignedBar` names both figures; `DashboardSummary`'s pool tile names the second, since the dashboard has no month on which a month ahead could otherwise be seen.

### The five conditions

The assignment terms cancel algebraically, so the invariant checks **coverage, not arithmetic**. Five conditions keep it true:

1. **`rows` is the union** of budget ids, the Uncategorized sentinel, and every id appearing in the ledger or in `assignments`. Filtering what the *grid* renders is fine; filtering what the *sums* cover is not.
2. **Both sides use the same period filter**, and the one assignment that falls outside it is carried by a term of its own. The pool subtracts *every* assignment: those at or before `P` against `Σ available`, those after it against `assignedAhead`. Drop the term and funding next month reads as money vanishing; leave the assignment out of the pool instead and the same dollar is offered twice.
3. **A split's parts are its legs and nothing else is.** `cumSpentCents` is built from the parts while `useAccountBalances` reads `amountCents`, so a division that did not add up would put the two cuts on two different transactions.
4. **A refund is counted once on each side, by different routes.** `toBeAssigned` uses `cumPoolIncomeCents` (uncategorised inflows only) while the right-hand side uses `cumIncomeCents` (every inflow) and a **gross** `cumSpentCents`. Netting refunds off the spend as well would subtract them twice; adding them to the pool as well would create money.
5. **A goal's `availableCents` is nothing but its own cumulative assignment**, which is what makes the third term cancel. **Give a goal any figure the ledger also sees and the identity breaks** — the whole reason a goal is not a category with an account behind it. A goal may *name* an off-budget account holding its money, and that figure is on **neither** side of the identity — off-budget money is not in `opening` and a transfer into it already left the budget as spending against a category — so it is reported beside `availableCents` by `useSavingsGoalEnvelopes` and must never be folded into `goalRows`. Fold it in and every invested dollar reads as a dollar the budget assigned and never spent.

### Beyond the identity

- **`opening` is the starting balance of the on-budget accounts**, reported separately rather than folded into `cumIncomeCents` — "income in August" is checked against payslips and an account opening is not part of it. Off-budget accounts are excluded: a 401(k) is not grocery money.
- **Rows carry `spentCents` and `refundCents` gross alongside the signed `activityCents`.** Use the signed one wherever the figures have to add up to the balance beside them (the dashboard table); use the gross ones only where the label says "spent" as a quantity.
- **Undated records count in every period's cumulative sums.** On a row they fold into `carriedIn`, never into this month's activity, so the displayed figures add up to the displayed balance.
- **Future-dated records are excluded** until their month arrives — hence the ban on mixing these figures with all-time ones.
- **An assignment is the one record it is legitimate to date ahead**, and `assignedAheadCents` is what keeps that honest — the term, its floor and the reason are above. Two consequences only visible here. A future assignment is **not bucketed at all**, so a category whose only funding is for a month not yet reached has no row in `rows` — the symmetry `goalRows` always had, which is why the note below about a harmless zero-valued tally bucket now describes nothing that happens. And the pool is otherwise **the same figure in every month**, bar the opening and income filters, which is what makes stepping `AssignIncomeModal`'s month a change of where money is *filed* rather than of how much there is to file.
- **Rollover is the closed form**, not a stored carry-in, so past periods are not immutable.
- **The day-one seed** in `AssignmentsContext` opens every category with an assignment equal to its existing spend — walking a divided receipt's parts, so each category opens at its own part — so a pre-existing ledger starts at zero envelopes with net cash unassigned. It runs in the **lazy default only**, and reads storage through `readStoredLedger` because no provider is mounted yet.
- **"Fill" tops a category up to its estimate outright**, ignoring carry-in, so sinking funds accumulate.
- A goal's `availableCents` is `goalAssignedThroughCents` and nothing else — no spend, no refund, no activity of any kind — which is precisely what makes the pool's goal term cancel in the identity.
- **A future-dated assignment must not seed a zero-valued row**, and `goalRows` is where that first had teeth: **its row set *is* its tally**, with no union with a record list to fall back on, so an assignment for a month not yet reached is skipped outright rather than filed as a zero. Bucket it first and a goal funded only in December appears in June carrying $0, which reads on the page as a goal that exists and has been given nothing rather than one that has not started. `rows` now keeps the same rule — it has the union to fall back on, so a *category's* empty bucket was harmless, but once a month ahead is an ordinary thing to fund the two had no business answering it differently.

`useSavingsGoalEnvelopes(period)` is the join on the other side: every goal from `SavingsGoalsContext`, against `goalRows`' figures for it — the same join `useDashboard` performs for categories and for the same reason: `useEnvelopes` knows the money, the goal store knows the goal, and neither may read the other. A goal with no assignments at all still gets a row, from a zeroed default, because the page's subject is the goals rather than the assignments.

```
available(g) = Σ assigned(g, p ≤ P)            ← out of the pool, the identity's term
held(g)      = Σ counted over the goal's off-budget accounts
saved(g)     = available + held
remaining(g) = max(0, target − saved)
```

**`heldCents` is the second place a goal's money can be**, and the hook is the only thing that knows both. `useHoldingOptions` (exported beside it) is the list it is chosen from and owns the one rule the store cannot: **off-budget accounts and nothing else**, because on-budget money is in the pool and every claim a goal has on the pool is its assignment — allowing one would need a second `reserved` term in the identity, and off-budget money needs none. Each account counts whole or at the part set aside, **never more than it holds** (`countedHoldingCents`), at `useNetWorth`'s valuation and never a second reading of the accounts — the rule `useEmergencyFund` keeps, deliberately not shared with it, since one counts on-budget cash held back out of the pool and the other counts money that was never in it.

**Both halves are reported and only `savedCents` adds them**, because a goal can hold both at once and the household is the only thing that can move one into the other: sweeping assigned money into the holding is a transfer out of the budget, which comes out of a *category* no goal may read, so a contribution left assigned afterwards sits in both piles. The row states the two apart — the holding under the goal's name, the assignment under the headline figure — so that is visible rather than silent. **Reported, never repaired**, `driftCents`' stance.

`remainingCents` is `target − saved − spent` **floored at zero**, so a goal met — or overshot, by a withdrawal that has not happened yet — never reads as owing money back.

**`spentCents` is the sum of the goal's `spending` statements, and it counts towards the target** — money spent out of the goal was saved first, so paying the venue leaves the wedding exactly as far along. It is read off the goal record and is on **neither side of the identity**: the money it describes moved out of `availableCents` into a category, where it is that category's activity like any other purchase. Fold it into `goalRows` and every paid bill reads twice. Spending is a move (`useGoalSpending`): goal −X and category +X in the payment's month, so the pool does not move. `spendableFromGoal` caps it at the least the goal holds from that month onward, since a backdated release lowers every later month too. Closing a goal out releases what is left to the pool in the current month and unfunds any month ahead, because an archived goal is off every form that could reach that money.

## Account balances (`src/hooks/useAccountBalances.js`)

The balance-sheet cut of the same ledger:

```
balance(a, P) = opening(a) + Σ inflows − Σ outflows, through P
```

Every dollar appears in both cuts — envelopes say what money is *for*, this says where it *is*.

**A transfer is the one record that moves two balances, and the one the two cuts read most differently**: here both halves are always real, while the envelope cut sees a transfer between two spend-through accounts as nothing at all. Each side is credited only if it still exists, so a transfer whose holding was deleted still leaves the account it left — the detached-record rule, applied to a record with two ends.

**A division between categories is invisible here**, and that is the seam the sum rule buys: what an account lost is `amountCents`, whatever the envelopes make of it.

**Derived, never stored**, which is what separates it from `balances` in `AccountsContext` — that is a hand-entered snapshot, the figure read off a statement. **This hook never reads one**, whatever account it belongs to: what the budget may spend, and what `/plan` reports beside each account, is the books' own arithmetic. A snapshot changes what an account is worth on the *net-worth* view and nothing else, which is the seam that keeps the envelope invariant true. Only on-budget accounts are offered in the transaction form, for the same reason as ever.

`accountBalancesAt(accounts, transactions, period)` is the derivation as a plain function; the hook is a `useMemo` over it. **It stays the only definition of what an account holds** — `useNetWorth` runs it once per month across a chart window rather than writing a second one that could drift from the figure shown beside it on `/plan`.

## Net worth (`src/hooks/useNetWorth.js`)

The join between the app's two ways of knowing what an account holds: the ledger's arithmetic, and the figure the user read off a statement. **Every account can carry both** — a month's snapshot is a record of the whole position, and the everyday accounts are half of it. What differs is the fallback, and the asymmetry is the design:

```
off budget    → snapshot at or before P, else derived   ← carries forward
spent through → snapshot for P exactly,  else derived   ← never carries
```

A 401(k) is worth what the market says, which no ledger can know, so May's figure is the best answer available in June. An everyday account's balance is live arithmetic: a statement read in May is the better answer *for May* — it catches what the books missed, and before the books opened it is the only answer at all — but standing in for June would freeze the account and hide every transaction logged since. So it answers for its own month and hands back.

An off-budget holding's derived figure is its opening balance **plus whatever has been transferred into it** rather than the opening alone — a real reading, but an incomplete one, since what it is missing is every dollar the market added. That gap is growth rather than a disagreement about the books, which is why `driftCents` is `null` for one.

**Snapshots carry forward, never back.** A holding last valued in May is worth May's figure in June and July — which is what makes a once-a-month update enough — but in April it is worth its opening balance. Propagating the earliest figure backwards would draw a flat line across years nobody recorded and present it as history.

### Drift, and settling it

Where both exist on a spend-through account, `driftCents` is the difference; **the hook reports it and never resolves it** (that gap is what a reconciliation is for), and `describeSource` is the one place the wording for where a figure came from lives — read by `UpdateBalancesModal`, which is the screen where a figure is entered and so the one where "carried from March" is worth saying. `HoldingsTable` is two columns of money and carries none of that prose; the only thing it still says about a gap is the offer to settle one.

**What closed the other half of that is `FixDriftModal`, not a change to the arithmetic**: the user says which record is right and the app writes an ordinary transaction for the difference — an **inflow** where the statement is higher (money arrived that nobody wrote down, so it is income waiting to be assigned) and an **outflow** where it is lower, which has to name a category like any other spending. A fourth kind of "adjustment" record would need a rule in the envelope identity, the balance sheet, the spending report and the drift figure itself, and the first consumer to forget it would break the invariant; two kinds the ledger already has need none.

It is dated **inside the statement's month** — month-end, or today where that is the current month, purely so the register shows no future date — because the gap is a disagreement about *that* month and a correction landing in this one would leave it standing. Nothing is reconciled by it: stamping the account checked is its own act, on the dashboard.

### `series` versus `chartSeries`

**`chartSeries` is `series` with every account's figure smoothed between the real snapshots that bracket it, and it exists for exactly one reason: the chart and its table twin.**

A holding entered in January and not touched again until June reads, in `series`, as January's figure held flat for four months and then a jump — the true record of what was actually entered, and what `current`, `changes`, `rows`, `tracked` and `driftCents` all need, since whether a holding is stale and what it is worth *right now* are questions about the real snapshots. `chartSeries` answers a different question — the probable shape of the account across the gap — and asks it of a spend-through account exactly as it does of an off-budget holding: a household whose ledger for that account only starts partway through its history leans on backdated statements for the months before that, and the chart bridges those the same way a 401(k)'s gaps get bridged, rather than showing the ledger's own (often empty) reading for months a statement already answered for.

It bridges only real data: before the first snapshot or after the last there is nothing to draw a line to, so the same carry-forward/opening-balance fallback stands for an off-budget holding, and the same hand-back-to-the-ledger fallback stands for a spend-through account — a lone reconciliation on an otherwise fully-tracked account never gets carried forward on the chart either, exactly as `series` never carries it. An account with no bracketing pair of real snapshots at all — the common case for a spend-through account whose ledger already answers for every month — is untouched by any of this.

**The line across an off-budget gap is bent toward where the money moved, not drawn straight — a spend-through account always takes the even ramp.** For an off-budget holding, an even month-by-month ramp is the right guess for a balance that simply grew, but a lump transfer out of a spending account (a down payment routed through a "Savings" category) makes an even ramp under-credit the holding for however long the gap runs, which reads on the total net-worth line as the money going briefly missing before "catching up": it never left, the account it was already in just hadn't been revalued yet.

`indexSavingsSpend` sums, by month, two things that are the same fact recorded two ways: a **transfer** whose destination is off budget (whole, since a transfer names the account the money reached and no division of it changes that), and the parts of an outflow filed under one of the **`TRANSFER_BUCKETS`** — savings *and* retirement, counting only those parts, so a $300 supermarket shop with $50 put aside is fifty dollars of evidence rather than three hundred — since both name money leaving a spending account on its way toward a holding rather than being spent. A transfer names the account the money went to, so it is evidence rather than a guess and its category is not consulted at all; a categorised outflow names only a category, so it stays a **proxy**. Both are counted because a household will hold both — years of contributions recorded as savings-bucket outflows before transfers existed, and transfers from the day they did — and dropping either half would take the weighting out from under exactly the history it was drawn for.

`interpolatedCents` weights the ramp by that figure instead of by elapsed months, still shared across every off-budget holding rather than attributed to one, since the proxy half cannot say which holding it was bound for. **Per-account attribution off the transfers alone is the next step here and deliberately not taken yet**: it would change the shape of every smoothed line, which is its own piece of work. Where a gap holds no transfer-bucket spend at all — most of them, where the whole change was market movement — it falls back to the even ramp.

**A spend-through account's own recorded activity is deliberately not used the same way**: the whole reason its gap needs bridging is usually that its ledger is left-censored — tracking only starts partway through — so its activity clusters wherever tracking happened to begin, not wherever the balance actually moved. Weighting by it would credit the first tracked month with the entire change and draw every earlier month flat, the exact staircase this function exists to remove; the off-budget signal avoids that because it reads a *different*, presumed-continuously-tracked account's ledger.

**A spend-through account gets one further bracket besides its own typed statements, and it is automatic.** `withCurrentBridge` reaches from its last backdated statement toward `period`'s own live ledger figure — `current`'s `derivedCents` for that account — but only where the ledger has *nothing at all* recorded in the months strictly between the two (`indexAccountActivePeriods` / `hasActivityBetween`; `period` itself is never part of that check, since the ledger is definitionally expected to answer for it). That is what lets a household backfilling a partially-tracked account see a smoothed line all the way to the present without re-typing a reconciliation every month just to keep the bridge alive — the typed figure is still what makes a reading *real* (`series`, `current`, `HoldingsTable` never see this virtual bracket, only the chart does). And it is what keeps a lone reconciliation on an otherwise fully-tracked account from being second-guessed: any real activity recorded since the last statement, however partial, leaves the account untouched and its accurate in-between months exactly as the ledger reads them.

`NetWorthPage` is the one place both `series` and `chartSeries` exist side by side: `chartSeries` feeds `NetWorthChart` and its table twin underneath, everything else on the page — `NetWorthSummary`, `HoldingsTable`, `UpdateBalancesModal` — reads the exact figures.

### Bands and the chart

`HOLDING_CLASSES` is a **coarser cut than `ASSET_CLASSES`**: cash / invested / property / debt, mapped by `BAND_BY_ASSET_CLASS` (total — an unmapped class falls to `property`), with any liability going to `debt` whatever it bought. Four bands is where a stacked chart stops being readable, which is also how many hues are validated against each other. `debtCents` is a positive magnitude (matching `owedCents`) with `0` written out rather than negated, since `-0` would travel into every comparison downstream — and `formatCents` normalises it too, because `Intl` renders `-0` as "-$0".

`useNetWorth(period, { months, spanKey })` returns `series` (oldest first, ending at `period`) and its chart-smoothed twin `chartSeries`, `current`, per-account `rows` (each carrying `valueCents`, the `derivedCents` it would have had from the books alone, and `driftCents`), and `tracked` — **every** account, the list the monthly pass walks, with the month each was **last** updated. That is the account's own latest snapshot and not the one in force at `period`, so stepping back to March does not make a holding updated in August look untouched since February. `needsUpdate` is true only of a hand-valued holding with no figure for the month: an everyday account with none is not waiting on one, it is answered by the books, and flagging it every month would cry wolf.

**The chart is cut by account, not by band.** `buildSlices(accounts)` is the series list — one per off-budget account, plus `SPENDING_SLICE`, a single band holding every account the budget spends through — and each series entry carries `values` keyed the same way. The everyday accounts are working capital passing through rather than holdings, and four of them are four thin bands that change places with each other as the month is spent, so they get one band between them; the account-by-account cut of *those* is the register.

Slices are ordered by band and then by the order accounts were added, **never by size**, or the stack reshuffles whenever two holdings cross and the reader reads the reshuffle as movement. `values` is signed and **a slice draws on the side its own figure puts it**, which is what lets the spending band be one series: a month whose cards outweigh its cash is genuinely negative working capital and draws below the baseline. The band totals (`cashCents` … `debtCents`) survive alongside for the summary tiles and `HoldingsTable`, which are the coarse cut.

### The ranges

`changes` is the headline change over each of `CHANGE_RANGES` — 1M, 3M, 6M, YTD, 1Y, 5Y, 10Y, ALL — each carrying `fromPeriod`, `fromCents`, `changeCents` and `changeBps`.

**Each entry also carries `months`**, which is what lets a single entry mean both things at once: `from(period, firstPeriod)` names the month the headline measures from, `months(period, firstPeriod)` sizes the chart's window, and picking one range picks both. The two are still allowed to disagree for the same key — "1y"'s window is the twelve columns ending at `period` while its `from` reaches one month earlier, to the month *before* the window opens, because a change "since a year ago" has always measured from outside the window it shares a key with (`netAt` is the fallback that lets it).

YTD measures from **December of the previous year**, the close of the old year, not from January's close, and its `months` is the same count the other way — however many months of the year have happened. ALL is the one pair that reads the ledger: both its `from` and its `months` resolve against `firstPeriod` — the earliest month anything is known about the books, an account's stated opening, a snapshot, or a transaction, computed once by `firstKnownPeriod` and returned alongside `changes` — capped at the same **120 months** (`MAX_WINDOW_MONTHS`) `10y` already sits at.

`percentChangeBps` returns `null` for a base of zero or a base on the other side of zero from where the household ended up — −$10,000 to $10,000 is not "up 200%" — and the page prints a dash; the dollar change is always shown, so refusing to invent a percentage costs nothing. Where both are the same side, the denominator is the base's **magnitude**, so a debt shrinking reads as up.

### The page's two controls

**The Net worth page has a month on it and the dashboard deliberately does not.** Half of what is on it is typed in, and the month it was typed for is the meaning of the figure. The stepper is also the whole backfill mechanism for a *missed month*: step back, `UpdateBalancesModal` writes to the month on screen, step forward. There is no separate mode, because a month entered late is the same record as one entered on time — which is why the modal puts the month in its title.

The stepper picks the month everything is *as of*, and one `SegmentedControl` (`SPANS`: 3M/6M/YTD/1Y/5Y/10Y/ALL, a subset of `CHANGE_RANGES` filtered by key — 1M is left off, since a one-column chart is not a shape worth drawing) picks how far back both the chart *and* the summary's headline change look. `useNetWorth` takes the picked key as `spanKey` rather than a bare `months` number (though the numeric option still exists and still wins when both are given, which is what lets `useNetWorth.test.js` size a window directly without going through a span at all) and resolves both halves off the one `CHANGE_RANGES` entry, which is what makes this one dial instead of two that could disagree.

### Backfilling a year at a time

**Arriving with years of statements is the other shape of the same job**, and it gets `BalanceHistoryPanel` — a `<details>` closed by default at the foot of `/plan`, holding a grid of a year's months × every account, committed through `setBalanceHistory`. Same records, different door: twelve open-and-close cycles of a modal is a chore, and the figures being entered are one series per account, which is a column. It sits on the page with no month in its corner because **every row names its own** — that, not the stepper, is what makes a grid safe there. It reads its own stores rather than taking props, since it needs the accounts, their snapshots and a per-month ledger balance; the last of those comes from `useNetWorth`'s own `series`, never from a second derivation.

**Blank cells are what both forms turn on.** A blank is a month nobody recorded, so what fills it is the fallback for that account — the books for one the budget spends through, the figure above for one valued by hand — shown greyed in the cell so the column reads as the series it is. Seeding a cell with the value it inherits would turn one statement into twelve records of it, which is also why `UpdateBalancesModal` starts a spend-through field **empty** with the ledger's figure beside it: only a typed figure becomes a record, so a monthly pass done on the 12th cannot freeze an account halfway through the month. In the grid, moving the window re-seeds every field, so an unsaved edit is lost — hence the warning beside the stepper while the form is dirty.

## Retirement (`src/hooks/useRetirementProjection.js`)

The only one of the nine that reads a store of guesses. It joins the plan's assumptions to three real figures: the ticked accounts at the value `useNetWorth` gives them (so a holding valued by hand counts at the figure the user entered), the expected income the spending target is a share of, and the **retirement** bucket the contribution defaults to — that bucket and not savings, plus the plan's own `pretaxContributionCents`, which no bucket can hold. **Nothing flows back** — no envelope and no balance moves because a scenario was explored.

**Everything is in today's dollars, and that is what makes the comparison honest.** The target is a share of what the household earns *now*, so the balance measured against it has to be in the same money: both rates are deflated once, `real = (1 + nominal) / (1 + inflation) − 1`, and the real rates are reported back so the page can print them beside the ones that were entered. Growing at a nominal 7% and measuring against a today's-dollars target overstates a thirty-year plan by about half, silently — it is the single most flattering error this kind of tool makes.

**Two rates, not one.** Money still being saved is a thirty-year bet; money being lived on is next year's groceries. The accumulation rate stops at the retirement age and the drawdown rate takes over.

```
need(S, n, d) = S × (1 − (1+d)^−n) × (1+d) / d      ← an annuity due
grow(b)       = b × (1+g) + C                        ← while saving
spend(b)      = (b − S) × (1+d)                      ← once retired
```

The withdrawal comes out at the **start** of the year and what is left grows, because that is the order retirement happens in, and `need` is the closed form that matches that recurrence exactly. **The tripwire in `useRetirementProjection.test.js` asserts it**: a balance of `need` on the first day of retirement is a balance of zero on the last. Any change to either half has to keep it green — that is the only thing keeping the closed form and the simulation describing the same plan.

**The series is the answer and the figures are read off it**, so the chart and the headline cannot disagree: `projectedCents` *is* the series' value at the retirement age. Only `need` and `requiredContributionCents` are closed forms, since neither has a simulation to read. Balances are floored at zero — a projection that draws a portfolio below the axis is drawing a debt nobody took on — and the year is called unaffordable only past `SHORTFALL_TOLERANCE` (a dollar), because carrying fractional cents through a sixty-year walk leaves an exactly-funded plan a fraction of a cent short of its final withdrawal, and a headline should not flip on that.

### The whole-balance-sheet walk (`src/netWorthProjection.js`)

Walks cash, invested, retirement, property and each debt forward beside the projection — from every account at `useNetWorth`'s value — and walks every enabled life event through it (`.claude/LIFE_EVENTS.md` is the plan).

- An event's income is spent before savings once retired, and a year it more than covers leaves the rest in cash.
- **A home is bought and sold in place at the start of its year**, sales before purchases so a downsizing can pay for the next one; each property is its own holding and each loan its own debt, because a sale names one.
- **A mortgage an event opens is paid on top of the budget** — today's budget cannot hold a payment that does not exist yet — where one already on the books is inside it.
- **A debt payment is inside the budget's spending while working and on top of it once retired**; debts run on a real rate and a deflated payment.
- Every loan's payment is fixed in nominal dollars and made at the **end** of its year, deflated by `elapsed − since + 1`; dropping the `+ 1` overpays every year and ends a thirty-year mortgage two years early.
- Assumptions sit on the same `retirementPlan` record: `incomeSource` (today's take-home grown by `incomeGrowthRateBps`, **or** gross `salaries` by age taxed by `workingTaxRateBps` — both kept, the source rule), `retirementTaxRateBps`, `cashRateBps`, `propertyRateBps`, and `debtAssumptions` per liability, kept there because a guess about a debt's future is not a fact about the account.

**Social Security is an income life event and has no field of its own** (`src/socialSecurity.js`, `SocialSecurityModal`). The need is already the discounted sum of the modelled retired-year draws *net of event income*, so a household that states its benefit is answered by the maths that was there — and a second term for it on the plan would be a second store of the same money, with a precedence rule to invent between them. What the module adds is the one piece of this a household cannot do in its head: the **statutory** claiming adjustment, 70% of the full benefit at 62 through 124% at 70, with the early reduction bending at three years out (5/9 of a point a month for the first thirty-six, 5/12 beyond). It is a constant and not an assumption, which is why it is not a field on the plan either. **The benefit itself is typed, never estimated** — it rests on thirty-five years of indexed earnings this app has never seen, and guessing it from one year of the income plan would be inventing a decision nobody made. The modal **seeds and does not keep**: the monthly figure and the claiming age are inputs to `annualCents`, not fields on the record, so re-opening it is not offered and the row's Edit button goes to the general event form — `seedFromGoal`'s contract, for `seedFromGoal`'s reason. Claiming later usually lowers the need despite the years it is not paid, which is the comparison the modal exists to show.

The page's outlook is `retirementOutlook`, read off that walk, and **`projectRetirement` is no longer what the page shows** — it stays as the pure function the reproduction tripwire holds the walk to. On course is the **liquid** money at the retirement age (cash and every investment, never the house); need is every retired year's draw — spending, debt payments and owning costs, less event income, with a sale's proceeds as a year that asks for less — discounted back as an annuity due at the drawdown rate and grossed up for tax on the pre-tax share. With one pot and flat spending that is `requiredNestEgg` exactly, which is why the old tripwire still means what it did.

**Any change to it must keep its three tripwires green**: the yearly identity, the reproduction of `projectRetirement` with only a retirement pot, and a pot of `need` ending at zero.

### Scenarios and the charts

**`resolveProjection({ plan, events, rows, budgets, expectedMonthlyCents })` is the hook's whole derivation as a plain function**, and the hook returns `resolveWith(plan, events)` beside its own result — the same books under a plan that is not in force. Every scenario row and every sensitivity row on the page is made by it, so a scenario differs from the current plan only by what it saved and never by a second derivation.

`ScenariosPanel` shows the current plan and each saved scenario as rows of the same headline figures under `ScenarioCompareChart` (net worth by age, one line per plan: the current plan in ink, each scenario in its slot's colour), with Use / Update / Remove per row and "In use" where the live plan and events match one field for field. Its sensitivity table reruns the current plan with each of the three rates a point either way.

"Year by year" draws the liquid series against the need. **`NetWorthByAgeChart`** under "Net worth by age" draws the whole balance sheet as a column per age — the net-worth bands in their measured colours, retirement money at full `invested` and other investments at half, debt below the baseline by `stackPaths`' sign rule, net worth as the line, the retirement age as a solid rule, and a marker under the year each event **begins** (every event a year holds is still named in its read-out and hit area).

**The Retirement page has no month on it, and that is deliberate in a different way from the dashboard's.** The dashboard is undated because everything on it is as of today; this is undated because nothing on it is about a month at all. A stepper would suggest the projection can be walked back through, and a plan has no history — only a current shape.

### Probabilistic outcomes (`src/retirementSimulation.js`, `src/historicalReturns.js`)

**The deterministic walk is one line: what the plan comes to if the market delivers exactly its assumed rate, every year, forever.** Monte Carlo and historical backtesting answer a different question — the spread of outcomes a market that actually varies would have left the household with — without being a second derivation of the plan: both read `resolveProjection`'s `walkInputs`, the exact object the deterministic `projectNetWorth` call is built from, and the only thing either engine changes is which invested-pot return each year gets.

**`projectNetWorth` grows the invested and retirement pots through one seam: `investedRateAt(age, working)`**, defaulting to exactly `working ? rates.growth : rates.drawdown` — today's flat-rate walk, unchanged, unless a caller passes something else. A Monte Carlo trial passes a draw; a historical cohort passes that calendar year's actual return. Nothing about the deterministic path's arithmetic changes because this parameter exists, which is what keeps its three tripwires where they were.

**A stock/bond mix is the plan's `returnSource: "mix"`** (`RETURN_SOURCES`, `RetirementContext.js`), the two-answers-kept rule once more: `growthRateBps`/`drawdownRateBps` stay exactly as typed under `"manual"`, and become *derived* under `"mix"` — `effectiveReturnRates(plan)` blends each phase's equity share (`equityShareBps` while saving, `equityShareRetiredBps` once retired) against `equityReturnBps`/`bondReturnBps`, nominal like every other rate here and deflated the same way, once, in `useRetirementProjection.js`. The deterministic figure under a mix is that blend held flat — the expected value of what the simulation draws around instead.

**Monte Carlo** (`runMonteCarlo`) draws a lognormal return per year for the equity leg and the bond leg — never below −100%, the way a real return never is either — correlated by `returnCorrelationBps` and blended by the phase's equity share (`blendedReturn`). One seeded PRNG (`mulberry32`) runs the whole batch, so a `seed` reproduces every trial, not just the first. **Zero volatility on both legs collapses a trial to the plain deterministic walk exactly** — the tripwire in `retirementSimulation.test.js` that ties this engine to the one `netWorthProjection.test.js` already guards.

**Historical backtesting** (`runHistoricalBacktest`) draws nothing: each cohort is the real sequence of annual real returns starting in one calendar year of `historicalReturns.js` (equity and bond, 1928–2025, NYU Stern/Damodaran plus multpl.com CPI, cited in full at that file's head), wrapping back to the dataset's first year if a plan's horizon runs past its last — the cFIREsim convention for a plan that outlives the data. A dataset with no year-to-year variance collapses the same way Monte Carlo's zero-volatility case does.

**Both engines report through one `aggregate`**: a success rate (the share of trials or cohorts with no `shortfallAge` before the end of the plan — exactly what the deterministic walk already means by a shortfall) and p10/p25/p50/p75/p90 of `liquidCents` at every age, which is what `RetirementFanChart` draws as two nested bands around a median line, the deterministic single path overlaid beside it.

**A batch runs only when asked**, via `runSimulation({ mode, trials, seed })` off the hook — unlike every other figure on the page, it does not recompute as the plan is edited. Five hundred trials or ninety-eight historical cohorts are each cheap; a "Run simulation" click re-running them on every keystroke of a volatility field would not be.

## Spending reports (`src/hooks/useSpendingReport.js`)

**The only one of the nine whose subject is a span of months rather than an instant** (`useGiving` has a span too, but its unit is the year; `useSavingsGoalEnvelopes` and `useEmergencyFund` are back to an instant). Every other view answers a question about one period; a report cannot be, because the whole point of it is that one month is not evidence. Whether a category is overspent is a question about August; whether the estimate against it is *wrong* is a question about the year.

It reads the ledger and `toSections` and **nothing else**. Not assignments — what was put in an envelope is a decision, and a report is about what happened. Not balances, not the openings: money the household already had is not money it earned.

```
netSpent(b) = spent(b) − refunded(b)          ← what the category cost
income      = inflows naming no category
netCents    = income − netSpent = Σ inflows − Σ outflows   ← over the window
```

The store's refund rule carries through unchanged: an inflow **with** a category went back to it, an inflow **without** one is income. That identity is the tripwire in `useSpendingReport.test.js` — both sides count every inflow exactly once by different routes, and netting a refund off spending *and* adding it to income would inflate both halves of the headline at once, which is the one error a savings rate cannot survive.

- **A divided receipt is ranked by where its money went, not by the receipt.** Each part is a leg against its own category and its own bucket, which is the entire reason a household splits one: a shop filed whole under essentials hides what it actually spends on wants. Each row's drill-in shows the **part**, since that is what the figures above it add up to, with the whole named beside it (`wholeAmountCents`) so a $20 line against a $124 register row reads as an explanation rather than a contradiction.
- **Undated records are in no report.** `useEnvelopes` folds them into every cumulative sum because a balance must account for every dollar; here the axis *is* the month, so placing one would invent the history the migration refused to invent. They are counted instead — `undatedCount` / `undatedSpentCents` / `undatedIncomeCents` — and the page says so, because money missing from a chart has to be visible as missing. **`undatedCount` counts records, not legs**: a receipt divided three ways is one row on the register and has to be one here, while every cent of it still reaches `undatedSpentCents`. Records outside the window, future-dated ones included, simply are not in it: this is a report of a span, not a balance at the end of one.
- **`REPORT_RANGES`** — 1M / 3M / 6M / 1Y / YTD / ALL / Custom, inclusive of both ends, capped at `MAX_WINDOW_MONTHS` (120, the net-worth chart's ceiling and for the same reason). Six are counted back from the end month and run to the current one; **"custom" is the one that names both of its ends**, which is what lets a report cover a calendar year, or a year that has already finished. It is a seventh preset rather than a mode — everything downstream reads the window the same way whichever produced it — and it is the only range that reads the hook's third argument, as "all" is the only one that reads the ledger. Every path is guarded back to a window of at least the end month, or every per-month figure would divide by an empty list; a pair typed backwards hits that guard, though the page's two fields bound each other so it should not arrive.
- **"1M" is the end month alone, and it is named "this month"** rather than "the last month" because a preset's end month is always `currentPeriod()` — the same guarantee "ytd" leans on for its year. The hook needs nothing else for it: a window of one month was already reachable through "custom", which is why `range.name` has always named a one-month window by the month itself. **What one month changes is what can be said over it, and both consequences turn on `averagedOverMonths === 1` rather than on the range** — so a year asked for over one month of records reads the same way, which is the same divisor and the same reason. A per-month figure then equals the total beside it, so `ReportSummary` prints each tile's figure once (the refund line, a different fact, stays) and `SpendingByCategoryTable` drops its **Per month** column outright, along with the "N of N months" each row would carry. And the estimate verdict goes with that column: over a single month the carry-over defence is back in full, so an average equal to one month cannot say the estimate is wrong, and `available < 0` on the dashboard stays the app's one reading of a single month.
- **The per-month average divides by the window, not by the months the category was active in** — that is what amortising *means*, and it is what makes a $1,200 annual premium comparable with the rent beside it. But it stops at the first month on the books (`averagedOverMonths`, `coverageStartPeriod`), or three months of records over a twelve-month window would understate everything fourfold and silently; the page prints the divisor when it differs.
- **Clipped at the far end too, by the calendar** (`coverageEndPeriod`, `coveredMonths`): a preset always ends at the current month and has nothing to clip there, but a custom window can be asked to run to December while it is still October, and counting two unlived months in the divisor would understate every figure on the page by a sixth. A month that has not happened is as empty as one nobody wrote anything down in, and for a better reason — so `PlanVsActualChart`, its table twin, `CategoryHistoryChart`'s trend and **`CashflowChart`'s net line** all withhold past `coverageEndPeriod` exactly as they withhold before `coverageStartPeriod`, while the plan's threshold is still drawn across both, since a standing figure neither began when the records did nor stops at today. The two ends are worded apart where the page explains the divisor, because "nobody wrote it down" and "it has not happened" are different facts and only one of them is fixable. Coverage is **global to the window, never per category**, or a category created last month would divide a one-off by one and report it as a monthly rate.
- **Rows are ranked by what was spent, not arranged by the plan.** The plan's order is answered twice already (`/plan` sets it, the dashboard reads it); the question here has an order of its own, which is the whole reason a report is worth having. Each row carries its heading, so the arrangement is still readable — it is just no longer the axis. A category with nothing against it is not a row.
- `buckets` is the same four shares `usePlanHealth` reports off the *estimates* read off the **books** instead, and that pair is the comparison. A category filed under no bucket (the Uncategorized sentinel, an id whose category was deleted) cannot be put in one of the four, so it gets a fifth segment rather than being dropped, or the split describes a smaller household than the headline does. The one asymmetry between the pair: `usePlanHealth` adds `pretaxContributionCents` to its retirement share, since a payroll deduction is money genuinely set aside; nothing here can, because a pre-tax deduction leaves no record in the books for a report of the books to find.
- **A transfer that moves no budget money is in no report**, not even in the undated count: paying a card is not an expense, and a report that counted it would say the household spent that money twice. One crossing the boundary **out** *is* spending against its category, exactly as the categorised outflow it replaces was. One crossing **in** is the single place this hook parts company with `useEnvelopes`, deliberately: there a withdrawal is money to assign because it can now be spent, here it is not income because the household did not earn it — draw $5,000 out of an emergency fund to fix a roof and this reports $5,000 of spending against what was actually earned, which is the month that really happened. The asymmetry is real rather than an oversight: the outbound half keeps faith with the shape the app already had, and the inbound half has no such precedent. `useGiving` keeps the same distinction, through its own `kind` test.
- `shareBps` returns `null` for a whole of zero or a negative one, and the page prints a dash — the same discipline as `percentChangeBps`.
- **`plannedCents` is the one figure here not read off the books**: every category's standing estimate, summed, including the categories that spent nothing — an estimate for a category that was quiet all year is still part of what the plan set aside, and dropping it would make the plan appear to shrink in exactly the months the household underspent. **Deliberately not `usePlanHealth`'s `plannedCents`**, which grosses the same sum up by `pretaxContributionCents`: a pre-tax deduction never becomes a transaction, so a plan that counts it drawn against spending that structurally cannot would show a permanent gap no amount of spending differently could close. `plannedWindowCents` is that figure times `averagedOverMonths` — the months the books cover, the per-month average's own divisor, for the same reason.
- **Each month also carries `buckets`, the same four-shares-plus-unfiled cut `buckets` reports over the window** — built in the one pass the rest of the hook already makes, since a second walk could disagree about the refund rule or about which month a record falls in. `BUCKET_SERIES` is the fixed list in the fixed order, **including the buckets a month spent nothing in**, because a stacked column colours a segment by its position and a list that dropped its empty entries would hand the same index to different buckets in different months. Each entry carries a `key`, since `bucket` is null for the unfiled segment and a null React key is a bug waiting for the next entry.
- **`days` is the window's spending by calendar day** — `spentCents`, `refundCents`, `netSpentCents` and the legs behind them, oldest first, one entry per day spending or a refund touched. Filled in the branch that adds to each month's spend, so **a month's days add up to its `netSpentCents`** by construction (`useSpendingReport.test.js` checks it from the seed). Income makes no day: a payday is not a day anything was spent. `SpendingHeatmap` shades it by the **quartiles** of the window's spending days, not by the largest, so one rent payment cannot wash every other day to the faintest step, and it draws days before `coverageStartPeriod` or after today as not in the books rather than as zero.
- **`incomeSources` is the window's income by who paid it** (`payeeId`, null for a record naming nobody), biggest first, filled in the same branch that adds to `totalIncomeCents` so the two cannot disagree. A payee's name is resolved on the page, as the drill-in's are.

### Where it came from, where it went (`src/cashflowSankey.js`, `CashflowSankey`)

**The window collapsed to one flow, starting with income on the left** → what was kept and the plan's buckets → the categories inside them. Income is one node: who paid it is carried on it (`payers`) for the read-out and the table twin rather than drawn as a column ahead of it, so the first thing the eye meets is the figure everything else is read against. The other two charts are the time axis and the ranking is the category axis; this is the only view that draws each answer as a *share of everything that came in*. It reads `useSpendingReport` and nothing else, so it follows the page's one control like every other figure there.

- **It conserves, by construction**, and `cashflowSankey.test.js` asserts it from outside: what reaches every middle node leaves it, and both ends add up to `totalCents`. The graph is built from the report's own identity rather than a second walk of the ledger — `netCents = income + Σ refunds-beyond-spending − Σ positive netSpent(b)`.
- **A band cannot have negative width, so a negative goes to the side its sign puts it** — `stackPaths`' rule one dimension over. A category refunded more than it spent enters on the left, under income, as **Refunds beyond spending** rather than being clamped (which would leave the two ends disagreeing by exactly that refund). What was left ends in **Kept**; a window that cost more than it earned instead has **Spent beyond income** under income, so the gap is drawn rather than hidden. **Sources meet destinations in order, each filled before the next** — income pays for Kept and the buckets top to bottom and whatever it cannot cover is met by the sources under it, so an overspend is drawn against the buckets at the end of the plan's order rather than smeared across all of them, in whole cents and with no band crossing another. Only one of the two is ever non-zero, and the card's header names whichever it is.
- **Kept sits in the bucket column, first**: it answers the bucket column's question (what the money did) and leads nowhere further, and below the buckets it would have their bands cut straight across it.
- Past twelve categories the smallest fold into **one node per bucket**, so a folded node still says what kind of spending it is and never has to cross another bucket's bands. A single leftover is drawn as itself. Below 480 units of width the category column is dropped and the buckets end the diagram; the table twin under it always carries every band, led by one row per payer.
- **Every node gets a slot tall enough for its two-line label, and is drawn at its true height inside it** — the slot is room for words, never extra money, so a band is always exactly as wide as its figure. Columns are then relaxed right to left towards the middle of what each node leads to, keeping their order, so a bucket sits beside its own categories.

### Plan against actual

**`PlanVsActualChart` is the second chart on the page**, and it cuts the same months along a third axis: the cashflow chart is the household against itself, this is the household against its own plan. Neither answers the other — a month can be comfortably in the black and still be nothing like the plan it was supposed to be, which is the month a budget exists to catch. It is also the honest answer to the radar chart people ask for: which buckets are over and under, read on an axis that does not distort area.

- **The plan is a threshold, so it is the one dashed mark here** — `RetirementChart`'s target line is the only other one in the app. A category's estimate is standing, the same figure in every month, so the plan's total is a flat line rather than a series with a shape; a second set of columns would invite reading it as data that moved, and a flat *solid* line would be in the same visual language as the grid. `scaleFor` **always leaves room for it**, or the one household the chart has good news for gets its threshold drawn off the top of the plot.
- **The column is stacked by bucket because a month over the plan is a fact and which kind of spending put it over is the reason** — the only part a household can act on. Four hues and a grey, `stackPaths`' sign rule as ever, so a bucket refunded more than it spent draws below the baseline rather than being clamped.
- **A month the books do not reach gets no verdict.** The window can reach back further than the ledger does — twelve months asked for against four months of records is the ordinary case — and those empty columns are months nobody wrote anything down in. Measuring them against the plan would report eight months of perfect discipline nobody lived, which would be the most flattering lie on the page. `coverageStartPeriod` is what withholds it, in the read-out, in the aria label and in the table twin alike. **The threshold is still drawn across them**, because the plan is standing and did not begin when the records did; it is only the claim about a *month* that is withheld.
- **A plan of zero is a plan nobody has written, not a plan to spend nothing**, so no line is drawn and the page says so with a pointer to `/plan`. A line along the baseline would read as a plan every month overshot.
- Income is deliberately not on it. It is the chart above's subject, and a second series on a second scale is the one thing a reader must never be asked to do.

**`src/bucketTones.js` is the one place a bucket's colour lives**, read by `PlanHealthSummary` (the plan's own split), `ReportSummary` (the same split off the books) and `PlanVsActualChart`. The whole value of those three drawing the same four shares is that a reader carries the colours between them, and a second copy of the map would eventually disagree without anything looking wrong in review. Three forms — `swatch`, `fill`, `text` — and **each holds whole class names, never a token to be interpolated**: Tailwind's scanner reads source for complete class strings, so a `` `fill-${colour}` `` compiles against some other file's literal and vanishes the day that file changes. `text` is only ever safe on `text-figure`, where `azure`'s 4.1:1 on `panel` clears AA and nowhere below it.

### The page

**One control, and it sets the window**; everything moves with it together, including the divisor under every per-month figure. Six of its seven options answer "how far back" and run to the current month, so the last column is usually a month still in progress — and the shortest of them is that month alone, the window a household reads most often, special-cased nowhere on the page (a single column is drawn as `barWidthFor`'s lone column, and the ranking and the bucket split are what one month was always going to be read for); the seventh reveals a **From** and a **To** month and names both ends, which is the report a preset structurally cannot give — a calendar year, a tax year, a year already finished. It is still one control and not two: a preset derives both ends and ignores the pair, and the pair answers for both ends while it is in force. The two month fields are **kept rather than cleared** when a preset is picked back up, and each bounds the other so the window cannot be typed backwards. There is deliberately still no end-month *stepper*: walking the window back one month at a time is a different gesture from naming the window, and the presets already do it from the other end.

**The chart and the table cut the same books along different axes** — the chart is the time axis, the table is the category axis over the whole window — and neither can answer the other's question.

`SpendingByCategoryTable` colours an average above the plan's estimate in `vermilion-ink`. **This is not a second definition of trouble contradicting the envelope view's one rule.** There the rule is `available < 0` and nothing else, because a category carrying money forward can exceed its estimate in one month by design. An average over the whole window removes exactly that defence — there is nowhere left for it to have carried from — so it says the estimate is wrong, which is a statement about the plan rather than about the envelope.

**Every row also carries `monthly` and `transactions` — the same window, one category deep** — for the drill-in (`CategoryDetailPanel`, drawing its chart through `CategoryHistoryChart`). The category's **name is a button**, not a heading, and clicking it opens the panel beneath the table rather than navigating away, because the ranking is still the context the reader picked that category out of; the same click closes it, so the row stays the one control.

Both are built in the one pass the rest of the row already needs, never a second filter over the ledger: `monthly` is `months` mapped to that category's own `{ spentCents, refundCents, netCents }`, aligned index-for-index the way `series` is, so a month the category was silent in is a real zero rather than a gap; `transactions` is every dated, in-window record that fed the row, newest first. A second, independent filter here could disagree with the ranking about which window a record falls in or about the refund rule, and the two would drift apart silently — reading the row instead makes that impossible.

`ReportsPage` owns which row is open (`selectedBudgetId`), not the table, because the selection has to survive the range control moving the window out from under it — a row can drop out of `rows` entirely when it did, and the page resolves the selection against the current rows rather than trusting it still names one.

## Giving (`src/hooks/useGiving.js`)

**The only one of the nine filed by calendar year.** Everything else here is filed by month, because a month is what a household plans in — but a deduction is claimed on a return, a return covers a year, and a goal that reset every month would be twelve different promises. Hence `YearStepper` beside `PeriodStepper`: same idea, different unit, and neither is a mode of the other. The year comes off each gift's own transaction date (`toYear`), derived and never stored, exactly as a period is.

**The ledger drives the join.** A tag whose transaction is gone contributes to nothing rather than to a figure with no row behind it, which is what makes the cascade in `deleteTransaction` a tidying rather than a correctness fix.

```
given(y)      = Σ tagged outflows dated in y  −  Σ tagged inflows dated in y
deductible(y) = the same sum over min(deductibleCents, amountCents)
```

- **A tagged inflow is money back from the organization and it subtracts** — the ledger's refund rule, applied to the one thing a returned donation changes here: it cannot be claimed in the year it came back. The row is marked `returned` and its deductible cell is not editable, because the sign belongs to the ledger and the way to restate one is to untag it.
- **The deductible portion is clamped to the gift, and the clamp is reported rather than repaired.** An amount edited down on the register leaves the stored figure out of date, not wrong — put the amount back and it means what it said again — so the row carries `statedDeductibleCents` and `clamped` beside the clamped figure.
- **An undated gift is in no year**, the same discipline the spending report applies to months: counted (`undatedCount`, `undatedTotalCents`) and said out loud, because money missing from a total that is going on a return has to be visible as missing.
- **A divided inflow is never income.** Naming no category is what makes an inflow earnings, and an inflow divided between categories names several — so it is refund all the way down, the same test `useSpendingReport` applies.
- **A share-of-income goal is a share of income *recorded*, not expected** — inflows naming no category, the report's own definition — so the target grows through the year, which is what stating a goal that way means. A refund is not income and is not part of what a tenth is a tenth of. `shareOfIncomeBps` is the same figure with no goal involved, and is what most households are actually asking.
- **`byRecipient` lists every organization, including the ones given nothing this year**: it is the list the page manages, not a ranking of the year's activity, and one that vanished in January would read as deleted. Gifts whose organization was removed get one unfiled bucket rather than a row each, because the parts have to add up to the total above them.
- **Nothing here is a tax calculation.** No limits, no carry-forward, no comparison against the standard deduction — those are questions about a whole return, and a figure that looked like an answer to them would be trusted as one.

`DonationList` owns the giving facts and the register owns the money facts: the organization, the deductible part and the receipt are edited on the row (blur for the figure, change for the select, as `TransactionRegister`), while the amount, the date and the description are read-only with a link to the register. **Untagging removes the statement, never the money** — the outflow still happened. `AddDonationModal` writes twice, ledger first, and **checks the giving fields before either write**, so the money is never the write that lands alone; the bound only it can check ("no more than the gift") lives there for the reason above.

## Savings goals (`src/pages/SavingsGoalsPage.js`, `src/components/SavingsGoalList.js`)

Medium-term saving in the envelope shape: a goal accumulates what is assigned to it, period by period, and `SavingsGoalList` reads like `CategoryLedgerTable`'s cousin — the target, what has accumulated, this period's contribution, what is left.

The contribution is edited **on the row**, commit on blur, the contract `CategoryPlanner`'s estimate cell keeps: a blank is zero (the row the store prunes), a refused figure goes back and says why *under* the row rather than beside it, so a rejected edit does not change the width of a column the eye is reading down. **A figure written with a leading "+" is added to what is already there** (`toCentsAgainst`, said once under the table and tied to every cell by `aria-describedby`) — the commonest edit to a standing figure is "another $250 into this", and the cell was asking the reader to do arithmetic the app was holding the numbers for. A leading **minus** is left meaning what it always did, a negative figure, because that is how money is pulled back out of an over-funded goal and the books already have that gesture. `COLUMNS` drives the header and the error row's `colSpan` together, `TransactionRegister`'s rule, and one column names itself off the period because what was assigned is a figure *for* a month while the target and the balance beside it are standing facts. `GoalMeter` is `TallyGauge`'s ticks with the tone reversed — full means funded — local for the same reason `FundedMeter` and `GivenMeter` are, and pointedly not a call to either of those.

**The page has no stepper, for the dashboard's reason**: it answers where each goal stands right now, not a month to walk back through. It also carries a **read-only** reading of "to be assigned", because a goal competes with every category for the same unassigned dollar and that figure has to be visible where the competition happens — read-only because assigning it is `AssignIncomeModal`'s job and there is one door into that pool, not two.

**`AssignIncomeModal` funds categories and goals off one submit, against one shared pool.** The live "remaining to assign" counter sums both prefixes (`assign:` and `goal:`) out of one `FormData` read, so a dollar typed into the wedding fund leaves the counter exactly as a dollar typed into groceries does — which is the honest reading, and would not be if goals had a form of their own.

**It also carries the month, which is how a month ahead is funded.** The `period` prop is the month it *opens* on; a `PeriodStepper` on the form moves it from there, and every figure on it — the rows, the goals, the pool, the title — is read at the month on screen. The control is here rather than on a page because the dashboard deliberately carries no month and this is already the pool's one door, so a second screen for a month not yet reached would be two forms competing for one dollar. `assignedAheadCents` is named in the same strip as the counter, and a month past the current one says so in words, because the figure is unchanged by stepping forward and the reader has to know that is the point rather than a bug. **"Assigned ahead" is the one wording for that figure**, shared with `ToBeAssignedBar` and the dashboard's pool tile — three screens naming one figure three ways is three figures as far as a reader can tell. The one use with **no** stepper is onboarding's starting-balance flow (`poolCentsOverride`), where the backdated month is part of what the override means.

A goal's **Fill** sets the period's contribution to whatever is left to reach the target outright, not to a monthly estimate: a goal has no per-period figure to top up to, which is what distinguishes it from a sinking-fund category.

Two stores mean this cannot be one atomic write, so **every figure on the form is validated before either call runs** — the same reason `AddDonationModal` checks the giving fields before touching the ledger. If the budget write lands and the goal write is then refused, the modal stays open with the error; that is the residual the two-store shape cannot design away, and it is why the all-or-nothing batch rule inside each store matters.

## The emergency fund (`src/hooks/useEmergencyFund.js`, `src/components/EmergencyFundPanel.js`)

The join it owns is between the two halves of the app that had never met: the plan says what a necessary month costs, the balance sheet says what is in the savings account, and an emergency fund is a statement about both at once.

```
monthlyEssentials = Σ plannedCents of every category counting as essentials
derivedTarget     = monthlyEssentials × monthsCovered
target            = derivedTarget, or the typed figure where that is in force
counted(a)        = valueCents(a), or min(part set aside, valueCents(a))
held              = Σ counted over the ticked accounts, at useNetWorth's figures
remaining         = max(0, target − held)
```

- **The essentials figure is `usePlanHealth`'s, not a second sum.** That hook already resolves which bucket a category actually counts as — the question needs the group beside the category, and `toSections` has done it once — and the whole value of deriving the target from the plan is that this figure *is* the one the plan's own split reports above it. Two sums over the same records would eventually disagree about a category whose group moved, and the page would show a fund that did not match the share printed over it.
- **Pre-tax retirement money is not in it, and that is a property of the bucket rather than a rule here**: `usePlanHealth` grosses `pretaxContributionCents` onto the *retirement* bucket, and essentials never sees it — a 401(k) deduction is not a bill that still has to be paid when the income stops.
- **Only on-budget accounts are offered or counted** — a buffer is cash reachable the week the income stops, so a card (a debt) and an off-budget holding (the retirement page's question) are filtered out in the hook, not just the picker. An id ticked before that narrowing is inert but kept.
- **What is held is `useNetWorth`'s valuation and never a second reading of the accounts**, the rule `useRetirementProjection` keeps for the ticked retirement accounts: a savings account reconciled by hand counts at the figure the household entered, one the ledger answers for counts at the books' own arithmetic. One month of window, because all it needs is what each account is worth today. Signed, so a debt someone chooses to tick subtracts, on the one sign convention the balance sheet uses everywhere.
- **Two readings of the same money, deliberately.** `fundedBps` is the share of the target that is there; `monthsHeldBps` is how many months of essentials it would actually cover, which is the sentence a household asks the question in — and it survives a target of zero, which is the ordinary state of a plan nobody has written estimates into yet. Both are `null` rather than zero where there is nothing to be a share of, the `percentChangeBps` discipline, and the page prints the month count as its headline for exactly that reason.
- **Choosing to type a figure and typing none is a target of zero, not a quiet fall back to the derivation.** The household has just said they want to state the figure; answering with the one they switched away from would hide that the field is still empty. The derived figure is still *reported* while the typed one is in force, and the other way round, because the panel shows the answer not chosen beside the one that is — which is the only thing that makes keeping it worth anything.
- Nothing here writes anything, and nothing reads it back. The fund moves when the plan moves or when a statement is entered, which is the whole argument for deriving it.

**The panel is on `/plan`, full width below both columns, and open rather than folded away.** Full width because it joins the two columns — the target comes out of the left-hand column's estimates and the balance out of the right-hand column's accounts, `BalanceHistoryPanel`'s placement and its reasoning. Open, unlike the three panels beneath it, because those are opened to set something up while this carries a figure meant to be met in passing, and a buffer nobody is reminded of is the one most likely to be quietly spent. Both figures commit on **blur**, the contract every panel outside a modal keeps, and each input is keyed on the stored value so a rejected edit — or one made on another device — re-seeds it.

## Card offers (`src/hooks/useCardOffers.js`, `src/components/CardOfferList.js`)

The rewards store knows what an offer promises, the ledger knows what was spent, and the join is what replaces the issuer's tracker that does not exist — the dining out already on the register is the dining out the offer counts, so nothing is typed twice.

`useCardOffers(today)` runs `offerProgress` per offer, values a target's bonus at `resolvedValuePer100Cents` (the household's own figure, as everywhere on the page), resolves names live so a rename follows, and sorts running offers first by deadline. **Nothing flows back**: an offer moves no envelope and refiles nothing.

On `/rewards` the panel **leads the page, ahead of the points**, because it is the one part that changes what the household does this week. `CardOfferList` takes rows and spies like `SavingsGoalList`; `describeOffer` is the one sentence each offer is read for, worded apart per kind because "$400 left" means *keep using this card* under a cap and *keep going* under a target. Its `OfferMeter` is local, the `GoalMeter` rule.

**The entry form reminds, at the moment the card is chosen.** `AddTransactionModal` reads the same rows and asks `offerNudges` (in `src/rewards.js`) which running offers a purchase bears on — open on its date, still with a use for spending, matching it by category (each part of a division) or payee — and says so under the category: on the offer's own card, the room or distance left **after** this purchase, or that it fills the cap and by how much it goes past; on any other card, which card would have counted it. Money out only. It is a sentence and never a check — nothing is refused or refiled for an offer.

The category and date are uncontrolled fields, so they are mirrored into `picked` by `syncPicked`, called from the form's `onChange` **and after every render**, since the re-seed, a draft, a payee's default and a remounted select all write the field without firing a change event; it bails out when nothing moved, so it cannot loop. Submit still reads the refs.

A credit is worded apart from the two bonuses wherever it appears — `describeOffer`, `rewardText`, `describeNudge` — because the same figure means the opposite thing: **$180 left under a cap is an opportunity and $180 left on a credit is money the fee already bought**, about to expire.

## What a card is worth (`src/hooks/useCardValue.js`, `src/components/CardValueList.js`)

**A household holds a points card and a cashback card at once, and the only question that matters is which one to reach for.** That question has an answer only in one unit, so every earn rate is reduced to `earnBps` — the share of the purchase the card gives back, at the household's own valuation of a point — and `rankCardsFor` orders the cards by it. `choices` is one row per kind of spending any card claims (each mapped category, each mapped payee, and "Everything else" where a card has a catch-all rate), best first, which is the `/rewards` comparison table; the register asks the same question of one purchase through `betterCardNote` in `AddTransactionModal`, and says something **only where another card beats the one in hand** — being told the card already in hand is the right one is noise on every entry.

The other half is whether a card is worth keeping:

```
net(card) = credits used + rewards earned − annual fee,   over the card's fee year
```

- **The window is the fee year, not the calendar year** (`feeYearWindow`): a card that renews in March is paid for from March and its credits reset then, so January-to-today would weigh nine months of credits against a whole year's fee. With no renewal date stated there is nothing to anchor to and the calendar year is both the usual reset for a credit and the one a household can check. `through` is always **today** — the fee is paid in full on day one and the credits are not, which is the asymmetry the panel exists to show.
- **Earnings are the ledger read at the household's own rates** (`cardEarnings`), through the same `spendingLegs` an offer uses: a transfer earns nothing, a division earns part by part, a refund takes its earnings back off the rate that paid them, and each rate's earnings are floored at zero before they are summed so one category's refunds cannot eat another's. Spending no rate covers is reported as `unratedCents` and claimed for nothing.
- **Credits are read off `useCardOffers`' rows rather than recomputed**, filtered to this card and to offers whose window overlaps the fee year (`offerOverlaps`), so the figure on the card's row and the figure on the offer's bar are the same figure. `expiredCents` — an ended credit's unused remainder — is the one figure on the page that is a loss already taken.
- **It is an estimate, and the page says so.** The issuer pays by merchant code, which the books do not have; this is what the rates the household typed come to on the spending the household recorded. Nothing flows back: no envelope moves, no row is written, no purchase is refiled.
- Rows lead with **the card whose fee is not earning itself back**, since that is the only one there is a decision to make about; free cards follow, best first.

## The dashboard (`src/hooks/useDashboard.js`)

`useDashboard(period)` joins `useEnvelopes` to `toSections` — the envelope figures, arranged the way the plan arranges them.

Rows carry `availableCents`, `budgetedCents`, signed `activityCents`, `targetCents` (the standing estimate) and `goalCents` (the balance the category is saving towards, set on `/plan`). **`goalCents` is `null` for a category with no goal — which is most of them, and all of the catch-alls** — and `null` renders as a dash rather than as $0. The same rule carries into the subtotals: `sumRows` adds up the goals that exist and returns **`null` when there are none**, so a band with nothing being saved for leaves the column blank instead of reporting a goal of zero.

**Every envelope row lands somewhere** — categories under their group, the Uncategorized sentinel and orphans in `otherRows` — which is what lets `totals` be compared against the headline figures. A row is dropped only when all three summed figures are zero — available, budgeted and activity; a goal is never one of the three, since a category saving towards something it has not been given yet is still nothing on the books.

**No category is judged against its own estimate.** The household budgets to an overall figure and does not mind how it is split, so a row is coloured by the sign of its Available figure and nothing else — green with money in it, grey at zero, red below (Actual's reading, not YNAB's target pill) — and an overdrawn row says "$X over" beside its name so red is never the only channel. There was a per-category funding reading (`fundingStatus.js`: underfunded / on track / well funded, a "$X short" beside the name, a key and an "N categories short" line); it came out because it answered a question this household does not ask.

**The one funding reading is on the whole month**: `useDashboard`'s `plannedCents` is every category's standing estimate summed off the **budgets** (not the rows, which prune a planned category nobody has touched — exactly the one that leaves the month short), and the "Budgeted this month" tile and the table footing's Budgeted figure turn **yellow** (`sulfur`) while this month's budgeted total is below it, the tile saying by how much in words and the footing to a screen reader. The panel's header also carries **Assign income**, the same `AssignIncomeModal` the register opens, since funding a category is the act this panel most often prompts.

### `CategoryLedgerTable`

Drives both its header and its body cells off one `COLUMNS` array, so the two cannot disagree about how many columns there are.

**The Available figure is also the control that moves it** — it opens `MoveMoneyModal` on that category, with a general door beside it in the panel's header. The figure rather than the row's name, because the money is the subject here and not the category (the name-as-button idiom belongs to the reports page, where the name really is what is being opened), and no new column either way, which would widen every row to carry a control used on one of them.

**The row clicked is always the destination, and the button says so**: clicking an envelope's own figure is the gesture of giving it something, so it seeds as `toBudgetId` whether it is short by four hundred or just short of the month, and **which neighbour gives it up stays the user's call** — the source is left blank, since guessing it would re-plan a category nobody asked about. An overspent row ("Cover …") is the one case with a figure worth seeding: the overspend is typed in, and **"to be assigned" is the source whenever the pool can cover exactly that** (otherwise blank like any other row). It belongs on the dashboard rather than on the register for the reason the to-be-assigned bar does not: what an envelope *holds* is a question about the plan and this page is where that question is answered.

**There are four columns — available, budgeted, activity, goal — and `targetCents` is deliberately not drawn**, even though `useDashboard` still puts it on every row for whoever wants it. The standing monthly estimate is plan-level detail: it is set on `/plan` and read there, it is the same figure in every period, and a dashboard is the at-a-glance page. Available leads because it is the figure the user acts on, budgeted and activity follow as the two things that moved it this month, and the goal sits last, away from the figures that come from the books.

### `ReconciliationList`

**Handed only the accounts the budget spends through** — `DashboardPage` filters `useAccountBalances`' rows on `spendsThroughBudget` before passing them. An off-budget holding is reconciled from the Net worth page, where its balance is actually used; nothing on the dashboard is assigned out of one, and its panel is about what the budget can spend. The balance on each row is derived and the reconciliation date beside it is not, which is exactly why the two share a row: a derived figure is only worth trusting as far as the last day someone confirmed it matched the bank.

**The row carries two controls because there are two sizes of answer** — "Accurate" for "it agrees", the dialog for when it does not — and the two wear the same weight, since which press is right is the statement's business rather than something a louder button could know. **"Accurate" is a check for today**: it stamps `reconciledOn` with today and goes disabled, reading "Checked", once that is what is on file. It was a checkbox, and a checkbox told two lies — it stood ticked through the whole stale window as though the agreement were live, and unticking it erased the date instead of taking back the day. Staleness is a reading of the date, and `isUpToDate` is its one predicate, behind the date's tone and the header's "N need checking" and nothing else; it tests `reconciledOn` rather than the age, because `daysSince` is null exactly when nothing has been reconciled and `null <= 31` is true in JS. A card shows neither control: `PayOffCardModal` already stamps it, so either here would be a second door to the same fact.

**The status line is sized to its content rather than `flex-1`**, which is what makes the wrap fall in the right place — a flex item that may not shrink below its own content pushes the action pair onto a line of its own the moment the two stop fitting, instead of the date being squeezed to three ragged lines beside it in the dashboard's narrow column. On a wider container they share one line, and no breakpoint had to be named to say so.

## The next paycheque (`src/hooks/useNextPaycheck.js`, `src/paySchedule.js`)

`useNextPaycheck(today)` is the join between the stored schedule and `src/paySchedule.js`, which is the calendar arithmetic as a pure module — no React, no colour, no wording beyond the one phrase both readouts share.

**Two families of schedule, and neither is a variation of the other.** An *interval* cadence (weekly, fortnightly, a custom day count) is the closed form `next = lastPaidOn + k × days` for the smallest `k ≥ 1` with `next ≥ today`: at least one period on, so recording today's pay does not point at today, and never in the past, so a schedule nobody has updated in months still names a real date. A *monthdays* cadence (twice a month, monthly) has no payday to step from at all — it is named days of the month, clamped to the month's length, so `31` is the last day whatever the month is. A day count cannot express it: twice-monthly pay is 13 days apart in February and 16 in July, and a "15.2 day" period drifts off the calendar within a quarter.

**A monthdays payday landing on a weekend is paid the Friday before**, which is what nearly every payroll does; bank holidays are not accounted for, so a payday beside one can read a day late. The rule is never applied to the interval family — those are anchored on a real payday, so every step of seven or fourteen days lands on the same weekday it did. Shifting means a month's paydays are **not all inside it** (a payday on the 1st can be paid at the end of the month before), which is why the search window starts a month early and why the candidates are sorted and de-duplicated after shifting rather than before — two days can clamp or shift onto one date, and one payday is one payday.

`describeSchedule` is the single phrase for what a schedule *is* ("every 2 weeks", "on the 15th and the last day"), shared by the dashboard tile and `PaySchedulePanel` so the two cannot describe one schedule differently. On the panel **the cadence comes first and decides what else is asked**, since neither family's fields mean anything under the other; it is also the one control there that commits on change rather than blur, because there is nothing to half-type.

## Recurrence (`src/recurrence.js`, `src/hooks/useUpcoming.js`)

**Which days a recurring bill lands on**, a pure module like `paySchedule.js` and `chartAxis.js`, and the third in that family — `cadence.js` answers what a recurring figure comes to in an average month (no dates in it), `paySchedule.js` answers when the household's own paycheque lands.

Four cadence names appear in all three tables by coincidence of English and **they are not to be consolidated**: the proof is the weekend rule, which `paySchedule.js` has and this must not — a payday on a Saturday is paid the Friday before because that is what payroll does, while a direct debit on a Saturday is taken on the Saturday, and lending payroll's courtesy to a water bill would report every bill due on the 1st of a month starting Sunday as due two days early.

**A schedule is anchored on one date and stepped**: every occurrence is computed from `startDate` rather than from the one before, which is what stops a bill due on the 31st from ratcheting down to the 28th forever the first time it passes February. Nothing here is stored.

`useUpcoming` is the join to the calendar, and it owns the one rule the arithmetic cannot state: **the window opens at the cursor, not at today**, so a bill due on the 1st that nobody entered is still on the list on the 9th, marked overdue — a list starting at today would let it vanish in silence, which is the one failure a bill reminder cannot have. One row per **occurrence**, not per schedule, since a weekly bill is genuinely four things to do this month and each is entered or skipped on its own.

## Loans (`src/hooks/useLoans.js`, `src/domain/amortization.js`)

**The eleventh cross-store hook**, and the one that joins Loans (the terms), `useNetWorth` (what the account owes and what the collateral is worth), Transactions (the payments the books recorded) and Retirement (`propertyRateBps`, the mortgage's collateral-rate default). `.claude/LOANS.md` is the plan this built from; the three decisions that shape it are decisions 1–3 there.

**Three readings of what is owed, kept apart because they disagree in normal use.** The **books' figure** is `useNetWorth`'s value for the account — the headline, and the only definition of what an account holds. The **contract schedule** is what the loan *should* owe if every payment was the contractual one, walked from the original principal, fixed for life, drawing the past half of the chart. The **projection** is the schedule walked forward from the books' figure *today*, at the current payment plus any what-if, so everything about the future — the countdown, the payoff date, interest to come, the what-if — reads it, and paying ahead or behind shows up the moment a statement is entered. `aheadCents` is signed, the books against the contract; the page says so past a payment's principal (`driftCents`' reported-never-resolved stance).

**Interest never touches the loan account** (decision 2): a row's `balanceCents` falls by principal plus extra and never by interest, because interest is money that left the household — an ordinary outflow from the paying account, not a credit to the loan. **The what-if holds no money** (decision 3): it reshapes the projection and moves no figure in the books.

**Recorded payments are counted, never matched to scheduled ones** — the `useUpcoming` rule: guessing which transfer was "payment 37" is guessing at intent and wrong the first time someone pays twice in a month on purpose. The schedule's count comes from the calendar, the books' from the ledger (transfers into the account), and both are shown.

`src/domain/amortization.js` is the arithmetic, pure and integer-cents like `netWorthProjection`: `paymentFor` (the annuity payment, `principal / months` at a zero rate), `amortize` (the walk — interest first, **the last row pays whatever is left**, capped at `MAX_SCHEDULE_ROWS` with `neverPaysOff` when the payment cannot cover the interest), `summarize`, `compareWhatIf`, `remainingAt`. **Biweekly is one extra monthly payment a year, modelled as exactly that**; daily simple interest is deliberately not modelled (the gap is cents, and an option nobody can check against a statement is a promise the app cannot keep). **`amortization.test.js` is the sixth tripwire** — see `TESTING.md`.
